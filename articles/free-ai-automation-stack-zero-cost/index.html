Free AI Automation Stack for Solopreneurs: What You Can Run at $0/month

Audience: solopreneurs, independent creators and small agencies who want to validate a revenue workflow before paying for software.

A “free stack” is useful only when it can move a real business outcome. The goal is not to collect dozens of free accounts. The goal is to connect a small number of services so a useful article can attract a person, capture permission to follow up, and route that person toward a real offer.

The lean stack

1. Publishing: WordPress.com Free. WordPress.com provides a free hosted site, and its current monetization guidance allows relevant affiliate links when the primary purpose remains original content rather than an affiliate-only site. WordPress.com also supports linking readers to external products. See the official monetization guidance.

2. Search measurement: Google Search Console. Search Console is free and exposes impressions, clicks, click-through rate and average position, plus query and page dimensions. That makes it the measurement layer for deciding which articles deserve rewriting rather than guessing from pageviews.

3. Email capture and digital sales: Kit Free. Kit's current free plan supports up to 10,000 subscribers, unlimited forms and landing pages, unlimited broadcasts, and selling digital products and subscriptions. Its paid automation features are not required to start collecting an audience. See the official Free plan details.

4. Checkout: Payhip Free Forever. Payhip currently lists a $0/month Free Forever plan with a 5% transaction fee. It supports multiple payment processors, including PayU and Razorpay for sellers in India, so the fixed-cost constraint can remain at zero while transactions carry the platform/payment fees. See pricing and PayU / Razorpay documentation.

5. Source/authority layer: GitHub. GitHub is useful for public technical assets, examples and transparent project artifacts. The blog's commercial checkout should remain on an appropriate commerce provider instead of treating GitHub Pages as the primary e-commerce surface.

What this stack does not promise

$0/month does not mean $0 transaction costs, unlimited reach or instant traffic. Payhip charges on successful sales; Kit charges transaction fees when commerce is used; payment processors can have their own fees. Search traffic can also take time to develop. The constraint should therefore be interpreted as “no recurring software bill before revenue,” not “no cost anywhere.”

The revenue path

  1. Publish a genuinely useful problem-solving article.
  2. Offer one free resource that is directly relevant to the article.
  3. Capture permission through an email form.
  4. Make one contextual recommendation: a product, template or verified affiliate offer.
  5. Send the reader to a real checkout or signup page.
  6. Record clicks, checkout starts and cleared payments.
  7. Rewrite the article when the data shows a specific weakness.

Why the article itself matters

Google's current guidance emphasizes helpful, reliable, people-first content and warns against scaled content created mainly to manipulate rankings. A zero-cost publishing system therefore needs editorial quality controls: specific claims, useful examples, clear sourcing, descriptive titles and enough original explanation to stand on its own. See Google's helpful-content guidance and spam policies.

When to pay

Upgrade only when evidence identifies the constraint. A paid SEO tool makes sense when search analysis is the bottleneck. Paid email automation makes sense when manual segmentation is the bottleneck. Paid hosting makes sense when site control, performance or commerce features are the bottleneck. Until then, keep the fixed-cost base at $0 and direct effort toward better articles and clearer offers.
