How to Monetize a Blog Without a Monthly Software Bill

The operating rule: keep the publishing layer free, pay only when a transaction or a proven bottleneck justifies it, and never confuse page generation with revenue.

A new blog has two separate jobs: earn attention and convert useful attention into an economic action. The easiest way to keep costs low is to avoid paying for every layer at once. A free publishing platform can host the content while a separate no-monthly-cost commerce system handles the transaction.

Model 1: original blog + external checkout

WordPress.com is a practical free publishing candidate because its current monetization documentation permits relevant affiliate links as long as the site's primary purpose is original content. This is important: the blog should be a publication first, not a thin page whose only purpose is sending clicks to merchants. WordPress.com's current monetization guidance also describes external payment and affiliate options.

For digital products, Payhip's Free Forever plan is currently $0/month and charges 5% per transaction. Payhip documents PayU and Razorpay support for India, including local payment methods, so an Indian creator can keep monthly software overhead at zero and pay transaction costs only when sales occur. See Payhip pricing and the PayU / Razorpay setup pages.

Model 2: blog + email audience

Kit Free can supply the audience layer. Its current free plan includes up to 10,000 subscribers, unlimited forms and landing pages, unlimited broadcasts, and digital products/subscriptions. This gives a blog a way to turn a one-time visitor into an owned audience without immediately buying an email SaaS plan. Kit's Free plan details explain what stays free and which advanced automation features require an upgrade.

Model 3: Blogger + AdSense later

Blogger is another free publishing route with an official AdSense path. Google documents that Blogger blogs can apply for AdSense and can use Auto ads after the site is eligible and approved. This is an additional monetization option, not a substitute for the product/affiliate funnel, because advertising depends on eligibility and audience scale. See Google's Blogger advertising guidance.

Why ads should not be the first target

Display advertising is volume-dependent. A new niche blog can be technically monetized while still producing negligible income if traffic is low. A higher-value editorial path is to solve a specific problem, show a useful workflow, then recommend an actually relevant paid product. That structure also fits WordPress.com's rule that original content must remain the site's primary purpose.

The $1,000/week target in unit terms

$1,000 per week is a target to operate toward, not a forecast. The arithmetic should be explicit:

  • 13 sales at $79 gross = $1,027.
  • 12 sales at $89 gross = $1,068.
  • 20 sales at $49 gross = $980.

These are unit-economics examples only. They do not imply that a new blog will generate those sales within seven days. The first week should therefore focus on proving the conversion path: real visitors → article engagement → outbound click → checkout start → cleared payment.

What to measure each day

Use Search Console for search impressions, clicks, CTR and average position. Then add article-level outbound click tracking and the commerce provider's confirmed sales data. A high-impression/low-CTR page needs a better search snippet; high-click/low-engagement content needs a better opening and promise; strong engagement/low CTA clicks needs clearer contextual offers; high CTA clicks/low payment completion points to checkout friction.

What not to do

Do not mass-generate articles simply to increase URL count. Google's current spam policy identifies scaled content abuse when many pages are created primarily to manipulate search rankings, including low-value AI-generated pages. A small cluster of genuinely useful, internally connected articles is safer than an artificial volume target. See Google's spam policies.

Do not publish unverified affiliate or checkout URLs. Do not claim revenue that has not cleared. Do not manufacture traffic, clicks, comments or reviews. Every commercial result should be traceable to a real external provider.

The operating stack

Discovery: Google Search and useful community distribution of the actual article. Publication: WordPress.com Free, with GitHub retained as the technical/source layer. Audience: Kit Free. Checkout: Payhip Free Forever or another verified no-monthly-cost provider. Measurement: Google Search Console plus provider-confirmed transactions.
