13-Week Cash-Flow Forecast for Solopreneurs

A profit number can look healthy while cash timing is uncomfortable. A 13-week forecast forces the operator to look at when money actually arrives and when commitments leave the account.

The basic structure

For each week, track opening cash, expected inflows, expected outflows and closing cash. Keep committed payments separate from uncertain opportunities. The forecast is useful precisely because uncertainty is visible.

Separate actuals from forecast

Do not overwrite last week's forecast with today's result. Preserve the distinction between what actually happened and what was expected. That creates a learning dataset instead of a cosmetic dashboard.

Use scenario bands

A simple base case can be paired with a conservative case and an upside case. The purpose is not to predict perfectly; it is to expose how sensitive the runway is to delayed receivables, unusual expenses or a missed sales month.

Decision rules

  • Flag any week where projected closing cash falls below the operating floor.
  • Flag any large inflow that is not yet contractually committed.
  • Review recurring costs separately from variable costs.
  • Compare forecast versus actual every week.

What a useful spreadsheet adds

The value is in the structure: clean inputs, visible assumptions, scenario calculations, weekly comparisons and an operator-friendly decision view.
